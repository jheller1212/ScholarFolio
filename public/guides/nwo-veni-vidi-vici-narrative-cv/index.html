NWO Veni, Vidi, Vici narrative CV: how to write the evidence-based CV

The pre-proposal for every NWO Talent Programme grant (Veni, Vidi and Vici) is built around an evidence-based CV: a narrative academic profile plus up to 10 key outputs. The committee judges the "quality of the researcher" on this CV alone, so it decides whether you are invited to write a full proposal.

What the evidence-based CV is

NWO introduced the evidence-based CV (EBCV) under the Dutch Recognition & Rewards programme and the San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA), which NWO has signed. The idea is that you substantiate your qualities with evidence instead of listing everything you have done. In NWO's own words, the why is just as important as the what.

The CV has two sections:

  • 2a. Academic profile — a narrative in which you describe your research line, vision, qualities and achievements, and how they exceed what is usual in your international peer group. In Vidi and Vici this section is split into 2a1 General academic profile and 2a2 Leadership and mentorship.
  • 2b. Key output — up to 10 outputs (articles, books, datasets, software, patents and other types) that best demonstrate your qualities. For each one you give the full reference, an optional link (preferably a DOI), the output type, at least one quality indicator, and a short motivation.

You are free to shape the academic profile however suits you: running text, sub-headings, bold or italic emphasis, or a list of achievements each followed by an explanation.

What you may and may not mention

The 2026 pre-proposal forms contain an explicit admissibility table. Committees are told to disregard anything that breaks these rules.

Not allowed

  • h-index, G-index, i10-index, and the total number of citations of an author across several publications
  • Journal impact factor, SNIP, SJR / SCImago Journal Rank, Q1, acceptance rates, and university rankings
  • Words that signal rank or reputation: “top”, “top-tier”, “prestigious” or “leading” journal or publisher
  • Totals of publications, acquired funds, grants and prizes
  • Lists of results without context, and any academic output that is not one of your (max. 10) key outputs
  • Hyperlinks anywhere except the URL field of each key output
  • Submitted, under-review or forthcoming manuscripts as key outputs (you may describe them as ongoing work in the academic profile, without naming the venue)

Allowed

  • Quality indicators tied to a single output: you pick one to three per key output (for example, citations of that one paper). If you use citations, NWO asks you to name the open database you took the number from.
  • Totals for supervision (for example, the number of PhD candidates you supervised)
  • Preprints and working papers as key outputs, if they are in an open repository and you do not name the intended journal
  • Context about circumstances that hampered your ability to show your qualities
  • An optional 50-word note on the publication culture of your field, above the key output list

How to write it, step by step

  1. Check your instrument and deadline

    Download the pre-proposal form for your domain from the NWO call page. Word limits differ between Veni, Vidi and Vici, and the explanatory notes inside the form are binding.

  2. Choose your 10 key outputs first

    Pick outputs that show your qualities and connect to your research idea, not just your most-cited papers. Outputs must be published, in print or unconditionally accepted before the pre-proposal deadline. Write full author lists (no “et al.”) so the committee can see your position.

  3. Add one to three quality indicators per output

    Choose indicators from the form’s drop-down list and explain why each one matters for that output. If you cite a citation count for one paper, say which open database it came from, for example OpenAlex.

  4. Write the motivation for each output

    Explain the significance of the output and your own contribution, especially for multi-author work. Motivations for several outputs may be combined.

  5. Draft the academic profile as an argument

    Describe your research line and vision, then back each claim with evidence and explain how it exceeds the norm in your international peer group. Refer to key outputs by their number in 2b rather than naming other papers. For Vidi and Vici, cover leadership and mentorship in 2a2.

  6. Run the admissibility check

    Search your text for h-index, impact factor, journal rankings, “top”, “leading”, “prestigious”, totals of papers or grants, and stray links. Then check every word count against your form.

Frequently asked questions

Can I mention my h-index in an NWO Veni, Vidi or Vici application?

No. The 2026 pre-proposal forms list the h-index, G-index, i10-index and total citations of an author as not allowed, and NWO asks committee members not to rely on such indicators.

Can I mention citation counts at all?

Only per output. NWO allows quality indicators that relate to a single key output, so the citation count of one paper can be used as an indicator for that paper. The forms ask you to name the open database the number came from.

How many words is the NWO academic profile?

For Veni 2026 it is 400 to 700 words. For Vidi 2026 and Vici 2026 the maximum is 1200 words, which in Vidi and Vici covers both the general academic profile and the leadership and mentorship part.

How many key outputs can I list?

A maximum of 10. You may list fewer, and you should delete empty output fields. You may not mention academic outputs elsewhere in the CV that are not among these key outputs.

Can I include a preprint or a paper under review?

Preprints and working papers in an open repository are allowed as key outputs, as long as you do not name the intended journal. Submitted or under-review manuscripts are not eligible as key outputs, but you may describe them as ongoing work in the academic profile.

When is the Vidi 2026 pre-proposal deadline?

NWO lists 3 November 2026 at 14:00 CET as the pre-proposal deadline for Vidi 2026 in all four domains (Science, SSH, AES and ZonMw). Invited applicants submit full proposals by 6 April 2027.
