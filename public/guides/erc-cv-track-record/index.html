ERC CV and Track Record: how to write the 4-page section

Every ERC Starting, Consolidator, Advanced and Synergy Grant application includes a Curriculum Vitae and Track Record of up to four pages per Principal Investigator. Since the 2025 calls the ERC no longer prescribes PI profiles; instead you present up to ten research outputs and your peer recognition, with room to explain them. At Step 1 the panel reads only this section and Part I of the scientific proposal.

What goes in each section

The Work Programme says the CV and Track Record should include personal details, education, key qualifications, current and previous positions, a list of up to ten research outputs, and selected examples of significant peer recognition. The ERC's recommended template groups this into three sections:

1. Personal details

Your education and key qualifications, current position(s) and relevant previous positions. The template also asks for researcher identifiers such as your ORCID iD.

2. Research achievements and peer recognition

A list of up to ten research outputs that show how you have advanced knowledge in your field, with an emphasis on more recent achievements. These can be publications, preprints, books, chapters, conference proceedings, datasets, software, patents and other outputs relevant to your field and project. You may add a short, factual explanation of each output's significance, your role in producing it, and how it shows you can carry out the proposed project.

Then list selected examples of peer recognition, such as prizes, awards, fellowships, elected academy memberships or invited presentations at major conferences, optionally with a short explanation of their importance.

3. Additional information

Optional context for the panel: career breaks, diverse career paths and major life events (including effects such as long-term illness or pandemic restrictions), and particularly noteworthy contributions to the research community beyond your own research.

What panels look for

The Work Programme asks applicants to use the ten outputs to illustrate how they have advanced the field, and tells senior applicants to focus on recent advances rather than lifetime achievements. Panels take your career stage and your CV and Track Record into account when judging your capacity to carry out the project.

The ERC's own guidance adds that you should explain your own contribution to each publication and how it affected the field, and use the Additional Information section to explain any gaps in your CV. Its slides also call the idea that you need publications in Nature, Science or high-impact-factor journals a rumour that is “NOT true”.

How to write it, step by step

  1. Start from the official Part B1 template

    Download the template for your call from the EU Funding & Tenders Portal. The CV and Track Record sits in Part B1, together with Part I of the scientific proposal; do not upload it as a separate document.

  2. Fill in personal details compactly

    Education, key qualifications and positions in reverse order, plus your ORCID iD. Keep this short: the space is better used on achievements.

  3. Choose up to ten outputs that tell one story

    Favour recent work and outputs that connect to the proposed project. Non-article outputs such as datasets and software count.

  4. Explain each output in one or two factual sentences

    Say what changed in the field because of it, what your role was, and why it shows you can deliver this project.

  5. Add peer recognition and context

    List selected prizes, fellowships, memberships and invited talks. Use Additional Information for career breaks, non-linear paths, and contributions to the research community.

  6. Check the four-page limit

    Only material inside the page limits is evaluated. Re-check the formatting rules in the template for your call before submitting.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the ERC CV and Track Record?

Up to four pages for each Principal Investigator, according to the ERC Work Programmes 2026 and 2027. Only material within the page limits is evaluated.

How many publications can I list in an ERC application?

The Track Record asks for a list of up to ten research outputs. They do not have to be journal articles: preprints, books, datasets, software, patents and other outputs are allowed.

Do I still need to fit an ERC “PI profile”?

No. Since the 2025 calls the ERC no longer uses prescriptive Principal Investigator profiles. You present your research achievements, peer recognition and any relevant career context instead.

Where do I explain career breaks?

In the Additional Information section. The template invites a short factual explanation of career breaks, diverse career paths and major life events, including those with no effect on eligibility.

When is the ERC Starting Grant 2027 deadline?

The ERC Work Programme 2027 lists 14 October 2026 as the Starting Grant 2027 deadline. The Consolidator Grant 2027 deadline is 12 January 2027 and the Advanced Grant 2027 deadline is 26 August 2027.
